# 3D — the ball redesign: what a basketball actually is, and what Minecraft will let you draw

Research notes behind `tools/ball_model.py` and the eight shipped skins. Everything below was
read before a single element was emitted; the decisive conclusions are marked **Decision**.

Scope: (a) real ball design, (b) how sports games make a ball read at 40 cm, (c) Minecraft
modelling conventions in 2026, (d) animating a held object with no client mod.

---

## 0. What was wrong with the shipped ball

The old ball was a cube: 25 boxes, six independent 128 px faces, one cross painted per face.
Consequences, in the order a player notices them:

1. it spun like a die, because the silhouette had four corners and the seam lines died at
   every cube edge;
2. the seams never joined up, so no orientation ever read as "a basketball";
3. the pivot sat at 0.55 blocks per half-extent but the corners reached 0.95, so the ball
   was drawn a third larger than the thing the physics collided with;
4. at 32 px — the size it is actually seen at — the cross was a black blob.

**Decision.** One silhouette, computed once, shared by every skin. Skins differ by palette and
material only. That is also what makes the skin-difference checks meaningful: if geometry can
vary, "these two balls are different" is not testable.

## 1. Real balls: panels, channels, ribs

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Panels on an NBA / NCAA ball | 8, cut by 3 great circles | [NBA Rule 1 §II](https://sports.stackexchange.com/questions/26292/what-are-the-dimensions-of-an-nba-basketball) |
| Channel geometry (NBA) | ~1.8 mm deep, ~3.2 mm wide | [Alibaba product insight: official NBA ball](https://www.alibaba.com/product-insights/what-basketball-does-the-nba-use-official-ball-details.html) |
| Seam / rib width (Spalding patent) | seam 2.54–6.35 mm, rib 2.5–7.6 mm (~4.7 mm typical) | [US 6,406,389 (Spalding)](https://www.freepatentsonline.com/6406389.html) |
| Channel depth range | 0.5–2.5 mm | same patent |
| Rib surface | smooth, **not** pebbled | [US 2013/0059683 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20130059683A1/en) |
| Cross Traxxion layout | two interlocking cross panels, i.e. two great circles at 90° | [2006-07 Spalding ball notes](https://www.collectible-supplies.com/) |
| FIBA Molten BG5000 | 8-panel | [Molten / FIBA ball documentation](https://www.winiwmicrofiber.com/) |
| Two-tone balls | alternate **panel** colours, not a painted stripe | [HuffSports](https://www.huffsports.com/), [SavvyScores](https://www.savvyscores.com/) |

Two photographed references were judged on the actual pixels rather than in prose: a Spalding
NBA official close-up, and a Molten BG5000 two-tone. Both show the same three things:

1. the channel is a **hard-edged flat band**, not a gradient — the colour change is one texel
   wide and dead straight;
2. the **panels are pebbled**, the rib is not;
3. the seam network is **three great circles**, producing eight panels — four "lune" pairs.

**Decision.** The geometry is an icosphere; a texel's panel membership is decided analytically by
`min(|x|, |y|, |z|)` of its own direction. That is the mathematical statement of "cut by three
great circles", it is one line of code, it is identical for all eight skins, and a unit test can
assert it. A hand-drawn seam map could only be as correct as the artist's last edit.

**Decision — rib, not groove.** A real ball *is* grooved: the panels are skived down to the rib.
In Minecraft a groove made of boxes closes up as neighbouring boxes interpenetrate at every
lower subdivision level, and it disappears entirely at 32 px. A rib that stands ~5 % of the
radius proud of the leather is unambiguous at every size and reads at icon scale. The rib keeps
a little form shading but **none of the pebble** — matching the patent's smooth rib.

## 2. Making a ball read at 40 cm: what 2K does that a naive sphere does not

The relevant problem is the same one NBA 2K and NBA Live solve: a ball on screen is roughly
32–132 px, and a uniform sphere under a single directional light reads as plastic.

- **Micro-texture breakup.** Real leather has a per-panel grain whose highlight breaks into
  thousands of tiny facets. In a cuboid-only format that has to be faked per face, not per
  texel: one hash-noise value per box, quantised into the palette ramp. This is
  `face_step()` + `_hash_noise()` in `tools/ball_model.py`, driven by `SKINS[key]["pebble"]`.
- **Specular that follows the surface, not the cube.** This is the trap that made the first
  iteration look like a heap of boulders: if you shade by *which face of the box* is showing,
  neighbouring boxes differ by up to 30 % brightness and you see the boxes. Shading must be
  dominated by the **radial normal** (82 % in the current renderer) with the face direction as a
  small bias (18 %). Every corner of the ball then darkens smoothly, which is what the eye reads
  as curvature.
- **A silhouette that is round at the smallest size.** An icosphere at subdivision 1 is 80
  faces — visibly octagonal at 32 px. Subdivision 2 (320 faces) costs ~130 KB of JSON per skin
  and is the difference between "basketball" and "low-poly boulder".
- **Contrast is the seam's job.** The rib is the darkest (or, on Glow/Fire, the brightest) thing
  on the ball at every size. On the Game Ball it is near-black against orange leather: at 32 px
  it is the only thing that survives.

**Decision — palette atlas, not a UV-mapped texture.** Minecraft gives every box face a single
*affine* UV rectangle. An arbitrarily oriented face cannot be projected onto a texture through an
affine rectangle without shearing it. Every attempt to map a seam pattern that way smeared, and
subdividing only made the smear finer. A 16×16 atlas of flat palette entries has one texel per
entry, so there is **nothing to smear**: the seam is where the geometry says it is, and texels
cannot disagree with each other. Rows: 0–1 leather ramp (32 steps), 2 channel ramp (16), 3–4
accent ramp (32, used only by Two-Tone).

## 3. Minecraft 3D conventions, 2026 (Paper 26.2)

- **Cuboid only.** `Polymesh` and `ItemModel` are not in the Paper 26.2 API jar; a custom mesh is
  not available to a server-side pack. Everything must be elements: `from`/`to` on a 1/16 grid.
  Hence a 320-box sphere rather than a smooth mesh.
- **Snap to 1/16.** The client rounds element bounds to 1/16 itself. Authoring off-grid leaves
  slivers that survive the rounding and show up as dark speckle. `_element()` snaps explicitly.
- **Overlap beats abutment.** Two boxes sharing a plane exactly z-fight; overlapping boxes always
  have one face clearly nearer, so the winner is deterministic. `OVERLAP_RATIO = 0.010` of the
  radius — enough to win, small enough not to swell the silhouette.
- **`item/generated` vs `item/handheld` vs `display`.** A `generated` model is a flat sprite, a
  `handheld` model tilts that sprite in the hand. Neither can carry a 3D element. A ball is a
  plain custom model whose `display` block does all the presentation work.
- **Item model vs `ItemDisplay` entity.** The inventory, the ground and the dropped-item render
  go through `display` transforms on the item model. A *world* object we position and spin — the
  ball in flight, the ball on the rack, the ball in the pull-up rig — is an `ItemDisplay` entity,
  which draws through its own transform (`display: fixed` by default). Two different code paths,
  so both have to be right.
- **Brightness on displays.** `ItemDisplay` takes a brightness override; a self-lit ball
  (Glow 14, Fire 15) also carries `light_emission` per element so the model glows in the hand
  and in the GUI where no entity brightness exists.
- **Animation lives in the texture.** A `.mcmeta` strip with `interpolate: true` and
  `width/height` is the only animation a server-side pack can ship. Fire is the only skin that
  earns one: a 16×128 strip, `frametime: 3`.
- **Animated texture + palette atlas interact.** A stacked strip is 16 wide and 8×16 tall, so the
  palette sampler must scale UVs by the *palette* dimensions (16×16), not by `tex.size`, or the
  Fire ball samples past the first frame and renders black.

## 4. Spin with a visible axis, without a client mod

A server can rotate an `ItemDisplay` every tick; it cannot interpolate between keyframes on the
client. Instead the *transform* is interpolated server-side onto a fixed tick grid, and the object
is redrawn each tick:

- the ball spins about a **fixed world axis** (the shot's own velocity cross-product), not about
  the camera, so the spin visibly has an axis;
- the model's `fixed` rotation is 45° in yaw, resting the ball on a **seam corner** rather than a
  panel face: at the default network angle a face-on ball hides its own pattern;
- because the rib network is three great circles, every 30° of rotation produces a different,
  recognisable pattern. That is what makes the spin readable rather than "a texture scrolling".

## 5. Numbers this design settled on

| Constant | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `SPHERE_RADIUS_BLOCKS` | 0.285 | == `PhysicsMath.BALL_RADIUS`; the ball must look like what it collides as |
| `VISUAL_BALL_SCALE` | 0.52 | `BasketballEntity` draws a unit model at this scale |
| `SPHERE_RADIUS` (units) | 8.77 | `0.285 / 0.52 * 16` |
| `SUBDIV` | 2 → 320 faces | round at 32 px |
| `OVERLAP_RATIO` | 0.010 | kills z-fighting, no silhouette swell |
| `CHANNEL_STEPS` | 96 per circle | 3.75° of arc per rib box: a continuous line |
| `CHANNEL_LIFT_RATIO` | 0.050 | clears the leather, ~5 % of radius |
| `CHANNEL_WIDTH_RATIO` | 0.062 | ~3.2 mm of a real ball, reads at 32 px |
| `CHANNEL_THICK_RATIO` | 0.045 | — |

All channel constants are **fractions of the radius**. They were absolute numbers first, and
shrinking the sphere from 18.4 to 8.77 units turned the ribs into a black scribble over the ball;
the preview render caught it immediately. Anything sized in model units inside a resizable object
has to be a ratio.
